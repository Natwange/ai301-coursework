## Who I am in threads

I am a student and an early open-source contributor learning how to investigate and reproduce issues carefully. I want my comments to be clear, specific, and evidence-based so maintainers can understand exactly what I tested without having to guess.

## Rules I write by

### Rule: Do not promise a fix or timeline

When claiming an issue, I say what I plan to investigate next without promising that I will fix it or finish by a certain date.

- Wrong: "I'll fix this issue by tomorrow."
- Right: "I'd like to investigate this issue and will report back with what I find."

### Rule: Be specific about what I tested

I name the relevant behavior or setup instead of using vague statements that could apply to any issue.

- Wrong: "I tested it and saw the problem."
- Right: "I tested the reported behavior with the environment and steps described below and recorded the result."

### Rule: Match confidence to the evidence

I only say that I reproduced an issue when my evidence actually shows the behavior described in the issue.

- Wrong: "Confirmed, I definitely reproduced the bug."
- Right: "I reproduced the reported behavior using the steps below; the observed output is included for comparison."

### Rule: Report unsuccessful results clearly

If I cannot reproduce the issue, I say that directly instead of hiding the result or pretending I succeeded.

- Wrong: "Everything seems fine, so the issue probably isn't a problem."
- Right: "I wasn't able to reproduce the reported behavior in this environment; I've included my steps and observed output below."

### Rule: Keep comments focused on useful evidence

I avoid filler and generic language and focus on information that helps another contributor understand or repeat my investigation.

- Wrong: "Hello! I hope you're doing well. I am very excited to work on this amazing project."
- Right: "I'd like to investigate this issue. I'll test the reported behavior and post my environment, steps, and results here."

## Things I never post

- A promise that I will fix an issue or finish by a specific date.
- A claim that I reproduced something when my evidence does not show it.
- A guess presented as a confirmed fact.
- A vague claim such as "it works" or "it is broken" without supporting evidence.
- Generic filler that does not help the maintainer understand my investigation.
